In "Love and Math," renowned mathematician Edward Frenkel reveals a side of mathematics we've rarely seen, suffused with all the beauty and elegance of a work of art. Part autobiography, part mathematical exposition, the book tells the story of Frenkel's journey from a young math prodigy in the Soviet Union—where he faced anti-Semitism—to becoming a professor at Harvard and Berkeley.

At its core, the book introduces the Langlands Program, often considered the "Grand Unified Theory of Mathematics." This visionary project connects representation theory and number theory, revealing deep links between fields that previously seemed unrelated. Frenkel passionately argues that mathematics is not just a dry set of rules but a way to perceive the hidden order and harmony of the universe, a source of profound love and insight.
