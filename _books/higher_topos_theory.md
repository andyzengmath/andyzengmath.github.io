Higher Topos Theory is a foundational treatise in higher category theory that introduces the theory of infinity-categories and infinity-topoi. Published in 2009, the book is considered a seminal work that has significantly impacted the understanding of abstract mathematical structures and their applications across various fields of mathematics, including algebraic geometry, homotopy theory, and mathematical physics.

The book primarily covers two interconnected topics: infinity-categories and infinity-topoi, with infinity-topoi being a specialized instance of the former. Lurie develops the theory of infinity-categories as a generalization of traditional categories, where morphisms between objects form a higher-dimensional structure. This framework uses the language of weak Kan complexes and quasicategories to rigorously define and explore these higher-dimensional structures.
